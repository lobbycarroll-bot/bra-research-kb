# Body Casting, Tape Doubles, and 3D Scanning

## Why Direct Body Capture Matters for Small/Shallow Busts

Standard dress forms and 3D body scanners both smooth away the critical apex-to-underbust transition — the curvature that distinguishes a shallow breast from a projected one. For a large bust, this smoothing is a minor error. For a small/shallow bust, this transition *is* the defining feature of the breast shape.

**Result:** Any pattern derived from a standard form or scan will systematically overshoot cup depth for shallow busts. Direct body capture is not optional — it's the correct starting point.

---

## Method 1: Plaster Bandage Casting (石膏包帯法)

**Quality:** Gold standard for fidelity. Captures the underbust break exactly.
**Source:** armeria-marine (JP professional patternmaker), Bunka Fashion Graduate University

### What it captures that nothing else does
- The exact boundary between bust apex and underbust
- The true width of the breast root (not the smoothed estimate from a scanner)
- Any asymmetry between left and right

### Basic process
1. Apply Vaseline or barrier cream + cling film to chest
2. Soak plaster bandage strips, apply overlapping layers over chest area
3. Allow to set (plaster heats while setting — normal)
4. **Cut the cast off** with bandage scissors — never pull it off
5. Allow to dry fully
6. Use the cast as a 3D mold for draping, or convert to flat pattern (see Bunka method below)

### Safety notes (from medical/orthotics sources)
- Remove body hair first, or use Nivea/Vaseline as barrier (hair gets ripped out)
- Never apply plaster directly to skin — cling film barrier is essential
- Never overtighten — breathing restriction is a real risk
- Plaster heats while curing — normal, but stop if hot/uncomfortable
- Cut the cast off with bandage scissors; never pull vertically

### Converting cast to flat pattern: Bunka Method
**Source:** Dr. Ito Yumiko, Bunka Fashion Graduate University (patented 2011)
**URL:** https://dricomeye.net/02_kenkyu/kenkyu_140120.html

This is the only Japanese academic source with a concrete body→pattern workflow:

1. Mark a grid on the skin using anatomical landmarks:
   - Reference points: 前腋点 (front armpit point) and 後腋点 (rear armpit point) + waist
   - Divide the torso into equal thirds/quarters — grid density scales with body size, not fixed centimeters
2. Place small round stickers at every grid intersection
3. Apply plaster bandage over the stickers; peel and dry
4. Glue torn washi paper with water paste to the **inner** (body-contact) surface of the cast
5. When dry, transfer sticker dimple positions as × marks, connect them to recreate the grid
6. Cut along grid lines and flatten → true-to-body pattern with quantified stretch/compression

**Why washi:** It tears cleanly along curves without puckering; it's thin enough to conform to the concave inner surface.

**Why stickers:** The sticker dimples remain visible through the plaster; they are the grid registration system.

**Key advantage:** The Bunka method produces a flat pattern that accounts for the actual surface area of the chest — including the underarm recess that laser scanners cannot reach (arm-lifted position adds ~20cm of surface). Grid anchored to anatomical points means it scales correctly to any frame size including 60–70cm underbust.

### Limitation
Cumbersome: time-consuming, subject gets tired, plaster is messy. Suitable for a one-time measurement session, not routine.

## Method 2: Tape Double / duct-tape body shell (ガムテープ体型コピー)

**Quality:** Good fidelity for the bust area; requires helper for full torso; solo possible for chest only with care.
**Source:** Japanese sewing teachers on note.com and X (yousaikoubou)
- https://note.com/kate_and_sister/n/n7dd2463a45ec
- https://x.com/yousaikoubou/status/852729559686324225

### Process
1. Wrap body in 3–4 thin layers of cling film (タコ糸 plastic wrap). Cover generously.
2. Apply packing/duct tape strips over the cling film — **tape never touches skin**
3. **Critical rule:** cut tape strips to ≤15 cm max
   - Longer strips twist, self-adhere, and cause you to overtighten — distorting the bust
4. For hollows (the underbust break): finely torn tape pieces, only 1–2 layers — never bridge the hollow with a flat strip or you lose the key curve
5. Draw center-front line, cup outline, dart lines, and any other construction lines on the shell while it's still on the body
6. Cut the shell off (scissors along back center or side seam)
7. Flatten to paper — the shell is the pattern

### Difference from traditional dress-form DTD
- This method produces a **flat pattern shell**, not a padded form
- The shell is the pattern; you don't pad a form
- For bust-specific work, you can do just the chest area without a full-body wrap

### Advantages for small/shallow busts
- Captures the actual breast root width and the underbust transition on Jum's body
- No form with wrong built-in geometry involved
- Can be done at home with helper and craft supplies
- The flattened shell shows where curvature occurs and how much dart is needed

## Method 3: Wacoal SCANBE Free 3D Scan

**Quality:** Good for measurements; NOT usable as a form or pattern source directly.
**Source:** Wacoal Japan retail service — https://www.wacoal.jp/scanbe/qa/

### What it does
- ~3-second body scan in a disposable paper bra (紙ブラ)
- Outputs: 20+ girth/length measurements, bra size recommendation, 3D avatar (Wacoal Carne app)
- Free at Wacoal Japan retail stores

### What it doesn't do
- Does NOT export a mesh
- Does NOT output a pattern
- The avatar is smoothed (same smoothing problem as all scans)

### Who can use it
- Underbust ≥55cm, cup ≤G — Jum's 65–70cm range works fine
- For accuracy: wear the disposable paper bra (not a padded bra — pads corrupt the capture)
- For target-shape work: scan in a padded bra to capture the desired shape

### Best use case
Ground-truth measurements for comparison against Japanese size charts. Free and accurate. Not a substitute for casting for pattern purposes.

**Availability:** Japan only; must visit a Wacoal Japan store. Check store locations via https://www.wacoal.jp/store/

## Korean/Chinese Equivalents

**Korean:** No hobbyist bust-casting tradition found. Life-casting is an SFX/prop craft (라이프캐스팅, 석고 몰드). Medical breast-form fitting uses casting but not for apparel patterns.

**Chinese:** Industrial use only. Patent CN1928923A shows cup dart computation from 3D virtual chest models. Commercial custom-bra services use handheld scanners (proprietary algorithms → cut patterns). No hobbyist cast-to-pattern workflow documented.

**Conclusion:** The Japanese sources (石膏包帯法 + Bunka washi method + tape double) are the richest and most actionable for Jum. Korean and Chinese equivalents exist industrially but not at hobbyist level.
