## Cup Type Overview

### Moulded Cups (몰드컵 / モルドカップ)
- Heat-formed foam compound pressed on a breast-shaped mould
- No seams; holds its shape independently
- Shape not visible from the front; must be viewed from the side to assess projection and upper-edge openness
- Produces a smooth silhouette under clothes; BP doesn't show
- Can make busts look one size larger (thickness)
- Lifted effect for drooping breasts because the shape is fixed
- **Korean note:** Most Korean retail bras are moulded

### Seamless Non-Moulded Cups
- Unfixed shape; conforms to natural breast shape
- Mostly bralettes, demi bras, some sports bras
- BP tends to show through; limits styling options

### Seamed Cups (절개 컵 / 絶開カップ)
- Most design flexibility; can target specific projection, depth, upper-coverage
- Seam placement relative to the Bust Point (BP) is the key design variable

## Seam Types and Their Properties

All seam types exist in **2-part** and **3-part** variants. More parts = more three-dimensional form = more projection. **3-part suits deep/projected cups; 2-part suits shallow cups.**

### Horizontal Seam (가로심)
- Seam starts and ends within the wire line (does not reach armpit or neckline)
- Strap attaches at top of cup
- Produces a relatively projected, cone-like profile from the side; rounder from the front
- Suits **balcony and U-shaped wires** (symmetrical arc)
- Common in: full-cup bras, strapless bras
- Suits FOB (full-on-bottom) breast distribution

### Diagonal Seam (대각선심)
- Seam starts at the armpit
- Usually paired with a **side panel** for lateral support
- Strap typically attaches at top of cup
- Used in: plunge bras, occasionally small-size full cups
- Variant — **curved seam**: the seam bends mid-route, allowing strap to attach to the lower cup; provides medial support (gathers breast toward center)
- Curved seam used in most 3/4-cup and balcony bras; upper cup material almost always stretchy lace

### Vertical Seam (세로심)
- Seam starts at the neckline, inward of the strap
- Good vertical support; creates lifted appearance
- **2-part vertical seam cups tend to be shallow** — confirmed by reviews of Cleo Juna, Cleo Marcie, and similar styles
- Because the vertical seam cannot cover the upper cup, mostly used in half-cup (½) designs
- Suits FOT (full-on-top) breast distribution
- **3-part vertical seam**: seam continues as a gentle curve to end (no sharp bend); upper cup is usually same material as lower cup (vs. curved/diagonal where upper cup is typically lace)

### Partial Seam
- Abbreviated seam that doesn't traverse the full cup
- Appears mostly in **small cups** and bralettes

### Side Panel
- An extra piece attached at the outer cup edge
- "Side-support bra" — gathers breast inward from both sides
- Usually combined with diagonal seam
- Examples: Panache Jasmine, Panache Envy

## Fitting Rules (Not Absolute — Guidelines)

| Breast type | Seam to try |
|------------|-------------|
| FOB (full-on-bottom) | Horizontal seam |
| FOT (full-on-top) | Vertical seam |
| Shallow | 2-part cups |
| Projected | 3-part cups |
| Wide-set / needing medial support | Diagonal/curved seam with side panel |

## Cup Coverage vs Volume: Full / ¾ / ½

**Critical distinction (frequently confused):** Coverage class describes how much of the upper chest the cup covers — NOT the cup size or volume.

Primary source: https://bra-research.netlify.app/articles/ambitionner-full-34-12-cup.html

| Coverage | Description | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Full cup | Covers the entire bust including upper chest | Base shape from which others are derived |
| ¾ cup | Full cup with ¼ removed from top | Most common in Korea, Japan, US |
| ½ cup (half-cup) | Full cup with half removed | Cup + band nearly in a straight line; U-wire common; strapless-friendly |

**The same cup volume applies to all three.** A full-cup 75C and a ¾-cup 75C have the same cup volume.

### Open vs Closed Upper Cup Edge (컵 상변 개폐)
Viewed from the side, the top edge of the cup either:
- **Open (열린)**: runs vertically or outward — accommodates upper-bust fullness, prevents overflow
- **Closed (닫힌)**: angles inward toward the chest — contains low upper bust, prevents gaping and BP show-through

**This edge direction is more predictive of fit than the coverage class.** A full cup with a vertical/open top edge suits full upper busts; a ¾ cup with a closed top edge suits low upper busts.

Moulded cups: check from the side — projection level and edge direction are both visible.

## Cup Shapes (Depth/Profile Taxonomy)

From armeria-marine (Japanese patternmaker):

| Shape | Japanese | Description | Suits |
|-------|----------|-------------|-------|
| Bowl | お椀型 | Round, projected, dome-like | Standard to projected busts |
| Fuji | 富士山型 | Conical | Varied |
| Plate/dish | お皿型 | Nearly flat, very shallow | **Small and shallow busts; recommended up to B–C** |

**Plate/dish cups (お皿型) are essentially absent from Western retail.** The Wacoal ウンナナクール is the canonical example. Despite the nearly flat cup, it creates a décolleté. Principle: the shape of the fabric against the breast root, not projecting depth, creates the appearance.

## Korean Market Observation
Korea produces and retails **almost exclusively 3/4-cup bras.** Most Korean consumers have limited direct experience with full cups or half cups. This is explicitly noted by ambitionner as a market default, not a fitting recommendation.

## Gore / Bridge Width (고어 / 鸡心)
The center panel between the two cups. Height and width affect:
- Cup spacing
- Whether the bra sits flat at center front or floats
- Tissue displacement toward center

**Chinese guidance:** Low gore (低鸡心) recommended for small/flat busts or protruding ribs. High gore for larger or wide-set busts.

**Small-frame failure mode:** In bras designed for larger busts, a high gore often floats rather than lying flat — because the breast tissue doesn't extend to where the gore expects it. A low gore or narrow gore is appropriate.

## Sister Size Principle
Same cup volume, different band/cup combination:
- 75C ≈ 80B (same cup volume, 80B has wider/shallower cup shape)
- 70D ≈ 75C ≈ 80B

Going up in band size without going up in cup size reduces the cup depth — which may feel more comfortable in a bra with too-deep cups, but does not solve the root fit problem and shifts the bra's center of gravity forward.
