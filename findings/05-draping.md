# Draping for Bras and Corsets

## Why Draping is the Right Starting Point for Small/Shallow Busts

Formula drafting bakes in assumptions about the relationship between bust apex and underbust depth. For the typical Western pattern base, those assumptions favour projected, deeper cups. Draping bypasses the formula entirely: the cup depth emerges from the actual breast shape, not from a depth formula tuned for larger bodies.

**For Jum:** Draping around her own body — or a tape-double/cast — will produce a shallower cup naturally, because her breast profile is shallow. No adjustment needed.

---

## Draping Cultures by Language

### Japan: Kakomi-seizu + Moulage tradition
- **かこみ製図 (kakomi seizu):** "enclosed drafting" — tracing from an existing garment or bra. Quick practical method. The existing bra acts as the pattern.
- **ムラージュ (moulage):** formal academic draping on a stand; taught at Bunka Fashion Graduate University
- Japanese home sewing has a genuine kakomi/draping culture; many tutorials describe tracing from existing bras

### China: Academic institutionalisation
- Chinese academic papers (e.g., Hans Publishers paperid=66067) teach bra draping on a stand using tape lines — princess seam line + bust line, darts spread across 4 panels
- This is formal, textbook-style draping; the academic literature is the richest Chinese-language source for this

### Korea: Thin at hobbyist level
- Professional ateliers (sewingladystudio) do draping; Korean academic programs include it
- Hobbyist video content almost nonexistent — draping dominates what exists, but the total volume is small

### West: Split by garment type
- Corset draping: well-documented, actively taught
- Bra draping: fragmented, mostly amateur tutorials
- Methodology is the same as Asian sources; gap is in accessibility and volume

## Method 1: Kakomi-seizu (Tracing from an Existing Bra)

**Source:** Sewing Atelier Menuet — https://menuet-dance.com/relax-bra/
**Applicability:** Non-wired / soft bra; also works as starting point for wired

### Process
1. Find a bra that fits well (or even approximately)
2. Lay flat, trace the cup shape onto paper, noting seam positions and dart apex
3. Add seam allowances
4. Use this as the draft — the sizing is automatically correct for the wearer

### Menuet's specific technique
- Drape fabric = 60綿天竺 (60-count cotton jersey), fuzzy side toward the bra — grips the bra so it doesn't slip while taking shape
- Trace both sides symmetrically; mark lace starting points and dart tips
- Separate かこみ製図 video documents M-size result on 2 sheets A4

### Advantage for small busts
Because the source bra is hers, the cup depth is automatically correct. No formula, no adjustment. The shallow cup shape transfers directly.

## Method 2: Draping on a Dress Form

**Sources:**
- YouTube (JP): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAQZ24pLqn8 (かこみ製図 video; same channel has サイズダウン pattern-downsizing video)
- Korean: sewingladystudio https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PK1G3tIqrLk

### Standard tape-line workflow (from Sparkly Sewist — Western source, same technique)
1. Mark tape lines on form: center front, middle cup seam line, cup outline/wire line
2. Pin muslin to the form, draping to the breast shape
3. Trace the piece outlines on the fabric from both sides
4. Transfer to paper via tracing paper; add seam allowance (~6mm / ¼")
5. Cut and sew trial version

### Critical limitation for small/shallow busts
**Standard dress forms have built-in ease and a projected bust.** The form's apex-to-underbust transition is smoothed. Draping on an unpadded standard form will produce a cup deeper than needed.

**Solutions:**
1. Pad the form with foam/batting to match the actual bust (下厚上薄パッド: thick at base, thin at top = adds width without projection)
2. Drape on the body directly (see tape-double in body-casting.md)
3. Drape on a plaster cast or tape-double shell

## Method 3: Draped Bralette from Tape Lines

**Source:** Sparkly Sewist — https://sparklysewist.com/how-to-drape-a-bralette-pattern/
**Type:** Triangle bralette; simplest entry point before wired cups

Key observation: author's form didn't match her size, yet the result fit — **draping self-corrects proportion** in ways formula drafting doesn't. The tape outline on the form determines the cup; a slightly wrong form still produces a closer result than a formula from a wrong block.

## Corset Draping

**Source:** Professor Pattern (Barcelona) — https://professorpattern.com
**Method:** Draw pattern lines directly onto masking tape applied to a corsetry mannequin; add panels; flatten to 2D.

### Corsetry stand requirement
Corsetry mannequins have negative ease at the waist and pronounced bust — the inverse of Jum's geometry. Her setup needs:
- Bust **padding** (wider/shallower, not projecting)
- Waist may not need negative ease for most bra-adjacent corsetry

**Korean example:** gygbs.tistory.com — Korean maker drafted an A-cup corset with all boning channels planned at drafting time. Construction-rich, minimal formulas.

## How to Drape a Cup from a Body Cast

If using a plaster cast (see body-casting.md):
1. Position the cast on a stable base
2. Pin muslin to the inner surface, smoothing to follow the breast shape
3. Clip into curved areas; mark the seam lines where the muslin lies flat
4. Remove, flatten, and trace — seam lines are now visible
5. Check seam lengths walk correctly; add seam allowances

**Advantage:** The cast IS the body; no form error involved. The resulting cup is geometrically correct for the shallow root shape.

## Pattern Downsizing (サイズダウン)

**Source:** Same JP YouTube channel as the かこみ製図 video

If a draft from draping is in the correct shape but slightly too large (or is from a larger existing bra), a サイズダウン (size-down) video documents the process:
- Reduce all dimensions proportionally from a reference point
- Keep wire channel seam lengths matched to the actual wire

## Chinese Academic Draping Method

**Source:** Hans Publishers paper (paperid=66067) — https://www.hanspub.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=66067

3D body scan → place on virtual form → tape lines applied digitally → pattern extracted → CLO3D virtual fitting → pressure maps → corrections with real numbers.

**Quantified correction example:** Low gore (鸡心中线 too low) + empty cup (空杯) fixed by extending BPE segment 7 → 8.5 cm and redrawing arcs MK' and KL.

This is the academic baseline: draping → virtual testing → measured correction. The same principles apply to physical draping + toile fitting.
