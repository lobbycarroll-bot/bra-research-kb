# Reverse-Engineering Shopping Guide — Current Fit Architecture

**Status:** 2026-09-06. Rebuilt from current measurements, physical fitting work and live purchasing constraints. Earlier band-65/75 recommendations and the Mésange-as-starting-point recommendation are retired.

## Confirmed working evidence

- Jum's current measurements are **78.0 cm snug underbust** and **81.5 cm standing/apex bust**.
- Wacoal Japan places 78 cm in band **80**. Her 3.5 cm standing difference is below nominal Japanese AAA volume; a retail cup label cannot be predicted confidently from these two measurements.
- Known wearable reference: Pepper size S, style BRA036, RN 174794.
- Best physical wire match found so far: **Emerald Erin France wire size 36**. `36` is a supplier wire number, not a 36-inch band.
- **France 36 and the Mésange pattern have already been tested.** The Mésange cups were far too large, the apex was too high, and the cradle/channel did not match the France wire perfectly. Jum had to redraft the cup and related geometry. The Mésange must be treated as a documented failed starting block, not a recommended solution.

The existing redraft and fitting observations are now the primary home-construction block. Preserve the original Mésange pieces, every redrafted piece and the fitted wire trace; measure the changes rather than restarting from the commercial pattern.

## What the fit suggests

The problem is not merely “small cup.” Current evidence points to very low volume on a shallow/splayed footprint. Cup volume, root footprint, wire number, wire spring, apex height and cradle shape must be separated.

Use two design layers:

1. **Anchored base:** the tested wire/cradle follows the root, the band supplies stability, and the natural cup has very little projection.
2. **Controlled silhouette:** removable cookies, graduated foam, side slings or vertical lift seams add chosen projection only after the base is stable.

## How to buy the Japanese items from the United States

### Recommended for Wacoal + Nissen + Japanese sheet music: one active buying proxy

Use a proxy that **purchases** the goods, rather than entering a bare forwarding-company address yourself. Nissen does not ship overseas and asks customers not to designate forwarding companies as delivery addresses. A purchasing proxy can first confirm whether it can place the order.

ZenMarket is the clearest current consolidated route:

1. Create one account and paste each exact product URL into its cart/request form.
2. Specify exact SKU, color and size in both the structured fields and notes.
3. Wait for the proxy to confirm price and availability before funding.
4. Add the sheet music and other Japanese items before parcel creation.
5. Consolidate only after everything reaches the warehouse.

Its published fee is generally ¥500 per item for ordinary shops (range ¥300–800), initial consolidation is included, and warehouse storage is free for 60 days. International shipping, Japanese domestic shipping when charged, and payment fees are additional. Official fee page: https://zenmarket.jp/en/fees.aspx

Proxy returns are not ordinary retail returns. ZenMarket can request one only if the seller agrees; after warehouse arrival it adds a ¥1,000-per-item cancellation fee plus domestic return shipping and seller charges. Once exported, international freight and timing make a fit return unrealistic.

### WorldShopping: simpler on Wacoal, worse for uncertain fit

If the WorldShopping cart appears on the Wacoal page, it is the lowest-friction option. Its current charges include a 10% purchasing fee on item price plus domestic delivery, then international freight and a ¥500-per-package handling fee. It can also accept manual purchase requests and can consolidate different merchants if support is contacted before the second payment.

The decisive limitation: WorldShopping explicitly does **not** accept returns or exchanges because the size did not fit, regardless of the original store's domestic policy. Use it only when the exact item/size is settled or the garment is valuable as a teardown even if it does not fit.

## Home-construction next step: use the redraft, not another stock pattern

No new Mésange purchase is needed, and its printed cup should not be used as the base. The next useful work is to quantify Jum's successful changes:

- overlay the original and redrafted cradle/channel;
- mark the France 36 free wire and the amount of wire spring used in the band;
- measure how far the apex moved down and inward/outward;
- calculate how much cup volume was removed by panel and by horizontal level;
- preserve a minimal natural cup as the control;
- copy only selected retail features—padding maps, side slings, lift sheets and strap vectors—onto that control.

Make later natural, foam and augmented samples on the **same corrected cradle**, changing one variable at a time. Ruby, Sapphire and other commercial patterns may supply seam ideas, but their cup/cradle blocks must not be presumed closer than the failed Mésange.

## Measurement record for every purchased bra

Before washing or cutting, record relaxed/stretched band; actual wire trace and spring; tip heights; gore width/height; cup-shell perimeter; horizontal/vertical cup depth; apex position; pad thickness on a grid; pad-pocket and sling shapes; strap vectors; elastic tension; layer order; and standardized front/side/three-quarter photos. Repeat photographs with no pad, supplied pad and experimental pad where possible.
