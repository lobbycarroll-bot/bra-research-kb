# Wire Geometry — Wire-First Fit and Jum's Current Baseline

**Status:** 2026-09-06. Physical fitting results outrank supplier charts and advertised pattern compatibility.

## Working conclusion

- The closest physical wire found so far is the **Emerald Erin France wire, size 36**.
- `36` is the supplier's wire identifier, not a 36-inch band. Jum's current 78.0 cm snug underbust falls in Japanese band 80.
- The France 36 is a useful baseline, not a perfect solved cradle. In the attempted Mésange build, the wire and pattern channel/cradle did not match perfectly.
- Jum's 81.5 cm standing/apex bust indicates very low standing volume but does not determine root width, apex height or cup shape.
- Cup volume, root footprint, wire length, free-wire curve, wire spring, cradle seamline and apex placement are separate variables.

## Confirmed Mésange failure

Jum already owns the France wire and Mésange pattern and built the combination. The stock Mésange was not usable:

- cups were far too large;
- apex was much too high;
- the France wire did not match the pattern's cradle/channel perfectly;
- the cup and related geometry required a complete redraft.

Therefore, “listed as compatible with the France wire” must not be translated into “fits Jum” or even “cradle is an exact trace of her particular wire/sample.” The original and redrafted pieces are valuable experimental evidence. Future work should start from Jum's redraft, not from another untouched copy of the Mésange.

## France 36 baseline

Official product: https://shop.emeralderin.com/products/france-underwires

Emerald Erin describes this family as shallow, splayed and short at center front and underarm. Its generic correspondence table is a pattern-selection aid, not a body-size diagnosis. For Jum, the relevant evidence is the physical try-on and trace.

Record the exact sample: free width, height, length, tip offset and asymmetry. Then record it sprung in Jum's corrected cradle. If the fit is only “best so far,” mark the remaining discrepancy by location rather than calling the wire perfect.

## Japanese wire taxonomy: useful shapes, incompatible numbering

Jimura publishes U, C and L families. These are curve families, not body types. Jimura's product numbers are wire sizes, not underbust measurements; its own example maps bra size 75D to wire number 85.

- U-type product and dimensions: https://www.jimura.jp/product/400
- Sample/selection chart: https://www.jimura.jp/product/419
- Wire catalog: https://www.jimura.jp/product-list/38

Jimura warns that wires with the same number can have different curves across manufacturers and recommends comparing curves rather than length alone. Its examples include Wacoal E70 to Jimura 85, Wing D75 to 85 and Peach John E75 to 80.

The sample page was updated 2025-12-15 to say the wire series is discontinued and will be sold while stock remains. These samples can still be useful overlays, but no Jimura label should replace the body trace or the France-36 baseline.

## Correct comparison method

1. Trace the body root and mark center-front and underarm limits.
2. Scan the actual France 36 wire unsprung on a 10 mm grid.
3. Scan Jum's corrected cradle seamline and original Mésange seamline separately.
4. Overlay at actual size, aligned first at the bottom point. Compare width, center-front height, outer-tip height, curve and total channel length.
5. Record the wire sprung inside each fitting band.
6. Reject any combination that sits on tissue, extends into the axilla, levers away from the sternum or distorts under band tension.

Supplier names such as “regular,” “vertical,” “plunge,” “France,” “U,” “C” and “L” are local taxonomies, not interchangeable standards.

## Draft from the corrected cradle, then control silhouette

The next controlled samples should use Jum's corrected cradle and the best wire position already achieved:

1. Preserve the redrafted low-volume natural cup as the control.
2. Measure how far its apex moved from the Mésange apex and how much volume was removed at each panel/level.
3. Copy that verified seamline into a cut-and-sew foam version only after the unpadded control is documented.
4. Compare an offset/vertical lift seam, then the same cup with a removable lower-outer cookie or side sling.
5. Change only one variable per sample.

This separates root anchoring from artificial-volume design. Commercial patterns can contribute seam and style ideas, but none should be presumed to provide the correct cup or cradle—even when a supplier lists the France wire as compatible.

## Regional evidence, narrowly stated

Several Asian-market products document low-volume strategies worth copying: Japanese AAA80/AA-A80 retail sizing, SATAMI's extra-wide A-cup wire and low gore, Sabina's vector-specific padding, and Korean pad/garment measurements. These are hypotheses and construction references, not proof of one “Asian” geometry.

## Measurement record

For every wire/cradle/cup sample, record supplier/family/number; free and sprung wire dimensions; cradle seamline and channel allowance; tip positions; band tension; tissue contact and wear marks; apex coordinates; horizontal and vertical cup depth; and actual-size overlays. Keep the original Mésange, each redraft and each fitting result under distinct sample numbers.
