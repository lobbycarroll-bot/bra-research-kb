# Wire Geometry — Wire-First Fit and Jum's Current Baseline

**Status:** 2026-09-06. This page replaces earlier claims that conflated wire numbers with band sizes or treated one regional body shape as universal.

## Working conclusion

- The best physical wire match found so far is the **Emerald Erin France wire in size 36**.
- `36` is Emerald Erin's wire identifier. It is **not** a 36-inch band and does not contradict Jum's 78 cm underbust.
- Jum's current 78.0 cm snug underbust and 81.5 cm standing/apex bust indicate a Japanese **band 80** and very low standing volume. They do not, by themselves, determine breast-root width or a final cup size.
- Cup volume, root footprint, wire length, wire spring and cradle shape are related but separate variables. A broad/shallow root can require a larger wire footprint while needing less than a conventional AAA80 cup volume.

The physical trace on the body outranks generic band/cup conversion tables.

## France 36 baseline

Official product: https://shop.emeralderin.com/products/france-underwires

Emerald Erin describes the France wire as shallow, splayed and short at both center front and underarm. It is intended especially for petite/small cups that find classic wires too narrow or tall. The wire is 24-gauge flat steel with nickel-free PET coating and is made in France.

Emerald Erin's generic correspondence chart associates wire 36 with several sister sizes (28E, 30DD, 32D, 34C, 36B and 38A). That is a pattern-selection aid, not a body-size diagnosis. For Jum, the reason to use France 36 is the reported physical root match.

Buy multiple identical pairs before development: one for the fitting band, one for a natural cup, one for a foam/vertical-seam cup, one for an augmented cup and one spare. Keep an unsprung reference wire for overlays.

## Japanese wire taxonomy: useful shapes, incompatible numbering

Jimura publishes several curve families:

| Family | Typical use | Important caution |
|---|---|---|
| U-type | Half-cup/strapless; more symmetrical arms | A curve family, not proof of a wide-root fit |
| C-type | Common three-quarter-cup curve | Compare the actual curve and tip heights |
| L-type | Shorter/asymmetrical family | Do not assume “L” means the correct small-bust wire |

Jimura's product numbers are **wire sizes**, not underbust measurements. Its own selection example maps bra size 75D to wire number 85. The U-type product page lists wire 65 at approximately 10.5 cm high, 6.4 cm wide and 18.7 cm long, while wire 80 is approximately 12.3 cm high, 7.6 cm wide and 22.4 cm long. Those labels must not be rewritten as “band 65” or “band 80.”

- U-type product and dimensions: https://www.jimura.jp/product/400
- Sample/selection chart: https://www.jimura.jp/product/419
- Wire catalog: https://www.jimura.jp/product-list/38

Jimura warns that wires with the same number can have different curves across manufacturers and recommends comparing curves rather than length alone. Its examples include Wacoal E70 to Jimura 85, Wing D75 to 85 and Peach John E75 to 80.

The sample page was updated 2025-12-15 to say that the wire series is discontinued and will be sold only while stock remains. If comparison is valuable, order actual-size paper copies or the available U/C/L sample sets promptly. Treat them as geometry references; do not presume any nominal Jimura number replaces the proven France 36.

## Correct comparison method

1. Trace the body root in a stable posture and mark center-front and underarm limits.
2. Photograph and scan the best-fitting France 36 wire unsprung on a 10 mm grid.
3. Overlay candidate wires at actual size. Align the bottom point first, then compare width, center-front height, outer-tip height and curvature—not merely arc length.
4. Test each candidate in a fitting band. Record both the free wire and the wire as sprung by the cradle/band.
5. Reject wires that sit on breast tissue, extend into the axilla, lever away from the sternum or distort when the band is tensioned.

Supplier names such as “regular,” “vertical,” “plunge,” “France,” “U,” “C” and “L” are local taxonomies. They are not interchangeable standards.

## Draft from the wire, then control silhouette

Use the France 36 root as the fixed base for the first experiments:

1. Draft or adapt the cradle to the wire with deliberate wire spring and suitable channel allowance.
2. Draft the cup seamline to the actual channel length.
3. Make a very low-projection natural cup first.
4. Keep the same cradle while comparing cup architectures: shallow darted stretch cup, cut-and-sew foam with an offset/vertical lift seam, and the same foam cup with a removable lower-outer cookie or side sling.
5. Change one variable per sample and record apex position, wire marks, cup gaping, pad thickness and garment silhouette.

This separates two jobs that commercial bras often blur: **anchoring the breast root** and **creating the desired apparent volume**.

## Western-versus-Asian claim: revised

It is not accurate to say Western suppliers offer only one geometry or that an “Asian wire” has one narrow shape. Several Western hobby suppliers sell multiple wire families, and Asian suppliers also use incompatible curve systems. The practical advantage of the Japanese source is its published dimensions and purchasable comparison samples, not proof of an ethnic geometry.

The useful regional evidence is narrower: some Asian-market bras explicitly alter the A-cup wire or padding architecture for low-volume customers. For example, SATAMI describes an extra-wide A-cup wire and low gore for a small bust with a wide breast base. This is a design hypothesis to test against Jum's body, not a rule about Asian bodies.

## Measurement record for each wire test

Record supplier/family/number; free wire width, height, length and tip offset; sprung width in the fitting band; cradle seamline; channel allowance; center-front and underarm tip positions; band tension; tissue contact; marks after wear; and actual-size front/side photos. Preserve the traced overlay with the sample number.
