## Core Problem: Western Patterns and Retail Run Too Deep

### Why flat formula drafting overshoots shallow busts
Source: armeria-marine (JP) — https://bra-research.netlify.app/articles/armeria-marine-bra-considerations.html

Industrial dress forms and CAD virtual bodies have **ease built in**. This smooths away the transition between バストトップ (bust apex) and アンダーバスト (underbust). For a projected bust, this transition is modest anyway. For a **shallow bust**, this transition is the defining curve — the tissue rises steeply from a wide root and falls quickly, rather than projecting outward. When ease flattens this curve:
- Flat formula drafts produce cups that project more than needed
- The cup doesn't follow the breast's actual surface
- Result: upper-cup gaping (カップ上辺浮き), floating gore, band riding up

### The underbust break is the key measurement no tool captures well
- 3D body scanners auto-smooth the curve (by design — "lumpy bodies are hard to drape on")
- CAD systems can modify: neck, bust, waist, hip — **not underbust**
- Only plaster bandage casting faithfully reproduces it

## Small-Bust Specific Fitting Rules

### Gore height (鸡心 / 고어)
- **Chinese guidance:** Low gore (低鸡心) for small/flat busts or protruding ribs
- High gore = more medial support, designed for larger/wider-set busts
- Failure mode on small busts: high gore floats, wire presses breast tissue (as shown in ambitionner fit-failure photos)
- Small-frame correction: prefer bras explicitly offering low gore, or adapt patterns by reducing gore height

### Upper cup edge (컵 상변)
- **Closed upper edge** (닫힌): cups inward toward chest — suits low upper bust (small/flat)
- **Open upper edge** (열린): runs outward/vertically — suits full upper bust
- Korean blogger note: most molded cups, when viewed from the side, reveal their upper-edge direction even if not labeled
- Small-bust selection: look for closed upper edges; full-cup designs with closed edges work well for low upper bust even though their name implies "big"

### Cup depth vs width
- For wide-set, shallow breasts: need **wide** cup, **shallow** depth
- Standard formula drafts assume a proportional relationship between width and depth that doesn't hold for this shape
- Solution: wire-first drafting (Chinese/Japanese industry method) — the cup is built from the wire arc, not from body measurements minus a formula

### Seam selection for shallow cups
- **2-part cups** → shallower projection
- **Vertical seam** → typically shallow (Cleo Juna, Marcie are cited examples)
- **Horizontal seam** → more projected
- For small/shallow busts: 2-part + vertical seam is the starting hypothesis

### Band and wire geometry
- Do not infer a narrow or wide wire from “wide-set”: spacing between breasts and width of each breast root are different variables.
- Western specialists do not offer only one wire shape. Emerald Erin currently sells Orange, Round, Bliss, France, Carmen, Plunge and Omega geometries; France is explicitly shallow, splayed, short and petite/small-cup oriented.
- Jimura U/C/L wires add documented Japanese comparison curves, but their wire numbers are not bra-band numbers. Select by a full-size overlay against the IMF, Pepper channel and known-good Emerald Erin wire.
- Compare arc length, lower curve, tip heights, asymmetry and spring—not width alone. See `findings/03-wire-geometry.md`.

## Plate / Dish Cup (お皿型) — Construction Idea vs Retail Evidence

The お皿型 concept is explicitly associated in the Japanese construction source with shallow/small busts. It works on a different principle from a strongly projected cup:

- Rather than creating projection, it contains tissue spread across a flatter or gently curved surface
- The fabric sits against the breast root's actual surface
- A broad, shallow surface can cradle natural width without demanding upper fullness

Do **not** automatically label a current retail bra “plate cup” unless the maker does so or the geometry has been measured. The earlier claim that Une Nana Cool broadly represents this construction—and that `ふゆうする` is its A/AA small-bust flagship—was not supported by the live catalog.

## Custom Drafting for Korean Body Types
Source: 한선미 속옷 디자인 연구소 (lina7792, Naver)

**Case study: 70B custom bra drafting**
- BP (nipple) sits 0.5 cm lower than Korean average
- Outer cup circumference ~1 cm longer than average
- Lower-cup pattern adjusted accordingly
- Symptom: red wire marks at outer cup edge — from using size tables alone without shape analysis
- Method: first-class inputs = BP height, lateral cup circumference; not just band + cup

**Implication for Jum:** Imported patterns (including Korean commercial patterns) are drafted to an "average" Korean body that may still not match. BP height and lateral cup length are shape inputs that need measuring separately.
